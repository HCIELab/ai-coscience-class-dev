Subject Name

Research with AI: Map a Field, Audit the Model, Build the Skill

Alternatives: "AI for Your Own Research: From a Hook Paper to a Skill That Does the Grunt Work" · "Build the Research Skill Your Field Is Missing"

Description

Please provide a brief catalog description: topics covered, background required, types of assignments, etc.

Six two-hour sessions on doing research with AI without being misled by it, ending in a tool you will keep using. In the first half you carry one well-cited paper from your own field through a field map, a citation graph in the browser that flags references that do not exist, and an audit of how later papers cite it against what it actually says. Every exchange with a model goes in an AI log graded for honesty. In the second half you turn one repeated task from your own research workflow into a Claude Code skill: instructions that encode your method, tested against ten cases you judged by hand, with the failure rate on the first line of the report and the cost per run logged. Students with Python go further: the skill calls a script at a model you choose, or becomes a one-page visualizer over your corpus. Ends with a demo day. Background: has read peer-reviewed papers in some field; no programming needed for the skill track. Uses MIT's Parley gateway. P/D/F on six checkoffs and a demo. A preview of 6.8500 AI-Assisted HCI Research (fall 2027).

Format

Six sessions, Tuesdays and Thursdays, 1 to 3 pm. Each is 25 minutes of concepts, 75 minutes of hands-on work, 20 minutes of checkoff. A worktime slot and a demo day in week 4. Every session appends to the student's AI log (asked, claimed, verified, result, did with it). The first three sessions produce the task the student will automate and the ten hand-judged cases it is tested against.

Project

Two tiers, same checkoffs, same demo. Chosen in session 3; moving down is free.

  • Skill track (pass bar). A skill folder that encodes one repeated task from the student's research workflow, tested against ten cases judged by hand, with a failure-rate report, a cost log, and a one-page tool card. No code
  • Script or visualizer track (stretch). The same skill delegating its model call to a script at a chosen model with validated output and retry; or a one-page browser visualizer over the student's corpus or a citation graph that calls the model and shows its answer next to the ground truth

A good task: done every week and boring (extracting, tagging, checking, formatting, triage); the right answer is checkable by opening the source; ten cases fit in two hours of hand work; a full run on the cheapest model costs under a dollar; the corpus is on disk on the day it is chosen.

Sample projects: citation auditor for one's own related-work section; reverse outliner that flags paragraphs with no argumentative function; field-map filler checked against the OpenAlex graph; user-study checklist extractor (N, tasks, measures, IRB); codebook applier on one's own interview data (existing IRB coverage only); methods table extractor for biology or chemistry papers; method section to parametric CAD sketch; review triager on public OpenReview reviews; dataset link extractor with liveness check; budget-aware model router.

Grading (P/D/F)

  • P: at least five of six session checkoffs by January 29, a demo with the skill or tool running on an unseen case, the AI log, the tool card
  • D: three or four checkoffs, or no demo. F: fewer than three
  • Automatic extension of any checkoff to the next session; none for demo day
  • Checkoffs test the shape of the artifact: quadrants non-empty, DOIs resolve, locators present, ten cases run, failure rate stated
  • Outside class: about two hours a week (reading, the ten-case ground truth, finishing checkoffs)

Policies

AI use allowed everywhere, logged everywhere, verified before kept; first pass by hand. Keys in the shell or settings, never in a repo. Cheapest model by default; spend checked before and after agentic sessions. Same rules as 6.8500 (guide).
