Guide

Reviewing and responding

Respond to every point. Comply with the ones that improve the argument. Argue the rest with evidence.

Reading as a reviewer

  1. Reverse outline at section level first. Where is the contribution sentence? What does the evaluation prove?
  2. Type each concern: claim, evidence, framing, writing, scope
  3. Ground every point in the text. "Consider limitations" is a failure of the role; "the claim in ¶2 s3 is not supported once related work is removed" is a review
  4. Name the one thing you would fix first, and why

Writing the review

  • Summary of the contribution in your words (so the authors know what you read)
  • Strengths, briefly and specifically
  • Concerns, typed and ranked, each with a concrete check the authors could run
  • Claims the paper cannot support, quoted
  • Your recommendation and how confident you are

Answering a review

  • Every point gets a response, even "we agree and changed X"
  • Comply where the change improves the argument, even if you disagree with the tone
  • Push back where the reviewer is wrong about a fact or asked for a different paper. Cite the section that answers them
  • Acceptance is usually decided by fewer points than you think. Find the decisive one

Confidentiality

Review packets used in this course are real reviews with identifiers removed. They stay inside the class. Do not share them, and do not paste them into external tools.
