Reading a field

Contextualize, not critique. Anyone can list limitations. Your job is the map.

Three-pass reading (Keshav)

  1. Five minutes. Title, abstract, introduction, section headings, conclusion. Decide: category, context, correctness, contribution, clarity
  2. One hour. Read the body, skip proofs. Study every figure. Mark unread references worth chasing. State the main thrust in your own words
  3. Deep read. Re-implement the paper in your head. Every assumption challenged. Only for the two or three papers that matter

Pass 1 on every paper you touch. Pass 2 on your hook paper and anything you cite for a claim. Pass 3 on the paper you are reproducing a figure from.

The four questions

  1. What came before. What did it build on, react to, borrow? Distinguish "cited by the paper" from "actually built on"
  2. Who disagrees. Which papers, groups, traditions contest the claim, the method, or the framing? Name them
  3. What happened after. Replications, extensions, reversals, uptake, quiet abandonment. How is it cited today vs how it described itself?
  4. Why this got attention. Timing, venue, writing, demo, controversy, author, or a real result. Attention and rigor are different things

The citation graph shows you what the summary left out. Node size is not quality; it is how often something was cited, which tracks writing and placement as much as correctness.

The weekly reading receipt

Five lines, twenty minutes, one paper a week: the claim in one sentence; the evidence in one sentence; one thing I would check; one sentence I would steal; one thing the model got wrong about this paper when I asked it afterwards.
