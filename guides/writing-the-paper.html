Guide

Writing the paper

A paper is an argument, not a chronology. Every paragraph earns the next.

The diary failure

"We first explored A. We then built B. Finally we evaluated C." Every sentence true; no sentence does anything for the reader. Test each paragraph: can its first sentence be rewritten as "This establishes that..."? If not, it is a diary entry.

Reverse outline

  1. Number every paragraph
  2. One sentence each: what it does for the argument, not what it is about
  3. Read only the sentences. Draw arrows for dependencies. Mark orphans and gaps
  4. Find the contribution sentence. What did every paragraph before it earn?

Do it first on a strong LBW, then on your own draft. The gap between the two skeletons is the lesson.

What each section holds up

SectionThe sentence it supports
Abstractthe contract: what the paper promises
Introductionnarrows to one gap; ends with the contribution sentence
Related workthe novelty claim: what prior work cannot do, in terms the contribution then delivers
Method or systemwhat was built or done, only as far as the claim needs
Evaluationthe "it works" sentence in the introduction; it must be able to fail
Discussionthe generalization and the boundary of the claim

LBW anatomy by genre

  • Fabrication or system: motivation, technique, demonstrations as evidence, technical evaluation of limits. Related work argues no existing technique achieves the property
  • Study: motivation, research questions, method, results, discussion. The claim lives in results; discussion earns the generalization
  • Theory or essay: the argument is the paper; sections are moves; evidence is literature, cases, analysis

The contribution statement sits in the same place in all three. What differs is the middle. Stop hunting for a universal template.

Four tags for any draft

E claim with evidence in the paper · C claim without evidence · F structural filler · G generic, true of any paper in the area. Tag your draft. Report the ratio. Then ask: could the evaluation have failed? Do the numbers trace to a procedure? Do the citations say what you say they say?

The weekly writing rep

150 words, fifteen minutes, tied to that week's reading: rewrite an abstract in one sentence; rewrite a diary paragraph as an argument paragraph; write the related-work paragraph that names the faction; write your contribution sentence and its failure condition. Swap with a neighbor; mark the sentence where you got lost.
